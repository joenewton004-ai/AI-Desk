## What it does
Creative software (Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere: Creative Cloud), document software (Acrobat: Document Cloud) and marketing software (Experience Cloud). Nearly all of it is sold as subscriptions.

## The 10-year record
- Revenue grew from **$4.2B to $23.8B**, and has risen every year.
- Gross margin has held at **85-89%**, and FCF margin at about **40%**.
- Return on capital has **risen** steadily, from 6% to 63%.

This is what a durable moat looks like in the numbers.

## Why it's cheap
- **Fear of AI disruption.** Generative tools (image and video models, AI design tools) could let people create without Adobe's software. The bear case is that customers stop paying for subscriptions when free tools give similar results. Adobe's AI-first ARR tripled to about **$500M**, but that's under 2% of its $27.1B total ARR.
- **Leadership change.** CEO Shantanu Narayen announced his retirement, and the CFO left for Marvell. The shares fell about 9% on the CFO news.
- **The share price is down about 40% from its peak.** It trades on roughly 9-11× forward earnings, against about 21× for the S&P 500.

## The case for the defence
- **The business is still growing.** Q2 FY26 revenue was a record **$6.62B, +13%**, and full-year guidance was raised to $26.5-26.6B in revenue and $24.35-24.45 in EPS.
- **Remaining performance obligations** (contracted future revenue) grew about 13%.
- **Firefly** monthly users rose from about 50M to over 90M in a year. HSBC upgraded the stock, arguing the AI fears are overblown.
- **Professional workflows** (file formats, team collaboration, enterprise contracts) are stickier than one-off image generation.

## How to think about it
At about 10× earnings with 40% FCF margins and buybacks, Adobe only needs to **hold its ground** to be a good investment. It doesn't need to grow fast. The investment question is narrow: **will the subscriber base and pricing hold over the next 3-5 years?**

## Check yourself
- Each quarter: net new Digital Media ARR and Creative Cloud seat counts. A decline would be the first hard evidence for the bear case.
- Who the new CEO is and what capital-allocation stance they take.
