## What it does
One of the world's largest gold miners, with mines in Africa, Australia and the Americas. It moved its listing from South Africa to the UK and NYSE in 2023, which is why its SEC history spans two company IDs.

## Why the screen likes it
Revenue grew 71% in 2025, margins are at records, and its forward P/E of about 9 is low. All of it comes from a gold price above **$4,400/oz**.

## What the 10-year record shows
- **Revenue was flat at about $3.3-4.6B a year from 2015 to 2023.** The jump since then is the gold price, not the company growing.
- **Operating margin swung between 1% and 43%.** In 2023 it was 1%; in 2025 it was 43%.
- **Return on capital was 1-16% in 2021-2024** (earlier years can't be calculated from its filings), then 40% in 2025. It only passes the screen's 12% average-ROIC gate because Yahoo's 4-year window starts at the beginning of the gold rally.

## What's happening operationally
- **Costs are rising faster than production.** All-in sustaining cost (AISC) was **$2,039/oz in Q2 2026, up from $1,666/oz** a year earlier. The drivers were labour inflation, gold-price-linked royalties, and fuel costs after the US-Iran conflict raised oil prices.
- **Q1 2026 production fell 7% year on year.**
- **The share count rose about 6.5% a year** (screen data), meaning new shares were issued (e.g. to fund acquisitions). Owners of a great business don't get diluted like that.

## How to think about it
A miner's profit is roughly (gold price − cost per ounce) × ounces. At $4,400 gold and roughly $2,000 AISC, margins are huge. If gold fell back to $3,000, the profit per ounce would roughly halve. **This is a gold-price view, not a quality-business view.** If you want gold exposure, an ETF like GLD avoids the operational and political risk of individual mines.

## What this teaches the screener
The great-business gates needed a **long-run history check**. The screener now has one, and it excludes AngloGold: pre-tax ROIC was under 10% in 2 of the 5 years where it can be measured.
