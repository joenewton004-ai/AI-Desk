## What it does
The world's largest diversified miner. **Copper** (Escondida in Chile and others) now supplies **54% of EBITDA**. **Iron ore** comes from Western Australia, where it's the lowest-cost major producer. It is also building a potash mine (Jansen) in Canada.

## Why the screen likes it
It has an operating margin of about 38%, an EV/EBIT of about 9, and strong 12-month price momentum (+62%) from the copper rally.

## The 10-year record (where the screen's verdict breaks down)
- **Revenue swings ±20% from year to year:** +46% in 2021, −17% in 2023, −8% in 2025. It hasn't steadily grown. Revenue has gone sideways since 2021.
- **Operating margin ranged from 10% (2016) to 52% (2022).** It moves with commodity prices.
- **The latest year was driven by prices.** Of the FY2026 EBITDA increase (+27% to about $33B), about **$5.6B came from commodity prices** and only about **$1.3B from operational improvements**.

## What it does well
- **Cost position.** Being the lowest-cost producer means it stays profitable at the bottom of the cycle, when others lose money. That's the closest thing to a moat a miner has.
- **Copper exposure.** Copper demand from electrification is a long-term tailwind.
- **Capital discipline.** Net debt is below $9B, and its dividend policy pays out a large share of profits (about $8.7B in FY2026).

## Risks
- **Prices.** A copper or iron-ore price fall would cut profits regardless of execution. China's steel demand drives iron ore.
- **Project costs.** FY2026 included a **$2.3B impairment on Jansen** (potash), a reminder that big mining projects often overrun.

## What this teaches the screener
Like AngloGold, BHP passed the 4-year gates because of a strong price period. The screener's 10-year check now excludes it, because its revenue fell in 3 of the last 10 years.
