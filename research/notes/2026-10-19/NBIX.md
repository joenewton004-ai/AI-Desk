## What it does
Neuroscience drug company. **Ingrezza** (for tardive dyskinesia and Huntington's chorea) is the main product, with 2026 guidance of $2.825-2.875B. **Crenessity** (for a rare adrenal disorder) launched recently and is growing quickly.

## Why the screen likes it
- Revenue is growing about 24% a year (+39% in Q2 2026, to $959M).
- It has net cash and a forward P/E of about 11.
- Revenue has risen strongly since Ingrezza was approved in 2017.

## The 10-year record
- **It went from no revenue to $2.9B.** Ingrezza's 2017 approval transformed the company.
- **Margins are only moderate for biotech:** operating margin of 8-24%, and pre-tax ROIC of 5-19%. That's well below Incyte or Halozyme, because Neurocrine reinvests heavily in R&D.
- **Weak spots:** the screen also flags heavy stock-based compensation (about 7.6% of revenue) and a rising share count. Both quietly dilute shareholders.

## Growth drivers
- **Ingrezza:** Q2 sales were $716M, up 15%, with record new-patient additions. Guidance was raised.
- **Crenessity:** $184M in Q2, and its prescriber base nearly tripled in a year. Only about 15% of diagnosed patients are treated so far, so there's plenty of room to grow. The company isn't giving formal guidance yet.

## Risks
- **Concentration.** Ingrezza is still roughly three-quarters of sales.
- **Drug price negotiation.** Medicare price negotiation under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) is expected to apply to Ingrezza from **2029**. Its direct competitor, Teva's Austedo, enters negotiation in **2027**, which could pressure Ingrezza's pricing even sooner.
- **Pipeline dependence.** Long-term value depends on the pipeline producing the next big drug.

## Check yourself
- How much of Ingrezza's sales are Medicare (most exposed to the 2029 negotiation).
- Ingrezza patent and exclusivity dates in the 10-K.
- Crenessity's quarterly trajectory: is it a $1B+ drug?
