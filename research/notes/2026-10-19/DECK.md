## What it does
Footwear brand owner. **HOKA** (running shoes) is about 70% of sales, and **UGG** is most of the rest. Most production is outsourced, so it needs little capital, which is why ROIC is so high.

## Why the screen likes it
EV/EBIT of about 8 and an FCF yield of about 9% are bargain-bin numbers for a business with a 60-130% ROIC and no net debt. It also buys back about 3% of its shares a year.

## The 10-year record (the important part)
In 10 years revenue tripled ($1.8B → $5.5B), gross margin rose from 47% to 58%, and operating margin from roughly 0% to 23%. Revenue has grown every single year, including through 2020. That steadiness is what separates a great business from a lucky one.

## What the market is worried about
- **HOKA is slowing.** Growth was in the high teens to 20s; it's now 7.7% (Q1 FY27). In May 2025 the stock fell more than 20% when HOKA missed growth expectations, and again in October 2025 on a soft sales forecast.
- **Tariffs.** Shoes are made in Asia, so US tariffs pressure margins and prices.
- **Brand fashion risk.** Footwear brands can fade quickly, and UGG itself had a long slump in the 2010s.

## Latest quarter (Q1 FY27, reported 23 Jul 2026)
- Sales rose 5.7% to $1.02B, with HOKA up 7.7% and UGG up 4.9%. Direct-to-consumer sales grew 13%.
- Gross margin was 56.4%, up from 55.8%.
- It bought back $338M of shares in the quarter.
- EPS guidance was raised to $7.35-7.50, and revenue guidance held at $5.86-5.91B.

## What would make this a buy (or not)
- **Bull:** HOKA settles into steady high-single-digit growth with stable margins. At about 10× earnings with 3% annual buybacks, that alone gives a solid return, so you don't need a growth rebound.
- **Bear:** HOKA is peaking as a brand (as many running brands have). Margins then compress as discounting returns.

## Check yourself
- HOKA's international vs US growth, and wholesale vs DTC trends.
- Inventory growth vs sales growth. Inventory rising faster than sales is the classic early warning for a fading brand.
