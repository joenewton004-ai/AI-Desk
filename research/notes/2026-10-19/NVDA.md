## What it does
It designs the GPUs and networking that power AI data centres, plus the CUDA software ecosystem that developers build on. Data centre revenue is now the large majority of sales.

## Reading the valuation correctly
The screen shows a **forward P/E of about 14 but an EV/EBIT of 36.** These aren't contradictory, just measured at different times:
- EV/EBIT uses the **last full fiscal year** (FY2026, revenue $216B).
- The forward P/E uses **analyst estimates for the next fiscal year**. NVIDIA's Q2 FY2027 revenue alone was **$96B**, guidance for Q3 is about **$108B**, and management has pointed to about **70% revenue growth in FY2028**.

So the stock looks cheap *only if* those forecasts come true.

## The 10-year record
- **Revenue grew from $4.7B to $216B.** Operating margin went from 16% to 60%, and FCF margin is about 45%.
- **But note FY2020 and FY2023.** Revenue fell 7% in FY2020 and was flat in FY2023, when operating margin dropped from 37% to 16% (crypto mining and gaming demand collapsed). **Semiconductors are cyclical.** The current boom is the biggest cycle ever, but it is still a cycle.

## Moat
- **CUDA** has more than 15 years of developer tools, libraries and habits, and is the main reason customers stay.
- NVIDIA builds complete systems (GPUs, networking, software), not just chips.
- A gross margin of about 71-75% shows exceptional pricing power.

## Risks
- **A spending cycle.** A handful of hyperscalers (Microsoft, Meta, Google, Amazon) and AI labs buy most of the output. If their capex slows, NVIDIA's revenue can fall fast, as FY2023 showed on a smaller scale.
- **In-house chips.** Google's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium and other custom silicon are designed to reduce dependence on NVIDIA.
- **Export controls** on China and other markets.
- **Size.** At a $5.2T market cap, the share price already assumes it stays dominant for years.

## Check yourself
- Hyperscalers' capex guidance each quarter. That's the leading indicator for NVIDIA's revenue.
- Gross-margin trend. A sustained fall would signal pricing pressure from competition.
