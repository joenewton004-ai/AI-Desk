## What the numbers really say

- **The 50/50 desk is mostly "half overnight, half cash."** The dip half is invested only about 5% of the desk on
  average, about one trade at a time. The rest earns T-bills. Its smooth line (8% volatility, −7.3% worst drop, and
  flat in the market's 10 worst months) comes from:
  - the overnight sleeve at half size;
  - a lot of cash;
  - not from a new edge.
- **Everything hangs on the overnight sleeve's trading costs.**
  - At 0.02% each way, the cost assumed in its research, the 50/50 desk has a Sharpe of 1.80.
  - At 0.05% it falls to 1.10, and to 0.39 in 2014-19.
  - The live paper fills so far have sold 0.45-0.68% below the official open, about ten times the sleeve's break-even.
    The market-on-open test (amendment e43f2959, judged around 2026-11-10) is measuring whether that's real or an
    Alpaca paper quirk.
  - Until it reports, putting more of the desk into the overnight sleeve is a bet on its costs.
- **It drops the two sleeves whose edge doesn't depend on tiny costs.** The quality screen and momentum hold for
  weeks to months, so costs barely matter to them.
- **The shared version (C) looks best on paper** (28%/yr) because it puts ~95% of the desk overnight every night. It's
  the most cost-sensitive of all.

## Verdict

Claude's recommendation was "not now": wait until the market-on-open test shows overnight sales fill close to the
official open, then raise the overnight share step by step. **The owner chose to go live on paper with variant C on
2026-10-27 (amendment c52ce018)**: quality and momentum are sold at the 2026-10-28 open.

These are monthly figures, 2014-06 to 2026-09, at 0.05% costs unless marked.

- **It's a cash fund with a small edge on top.** Even the busiest version has only 11-32% of the money in dips on
  average; the rest earns T-bills.
- **Return: about 6-8% a year.** That's well below SPY (13.7%), the old desk (21.5%) and the current
  overnight-heavy desk (28% at 0.02% overnight costs).
- **Very small drawdowns** (−3% to −7%), because it's mostly cash and the dips rarely happen in crashes.
- **Loosening the rule doesn't fix it.** RSI < 35 roughly triples the dips but halves the profit per dip, and at
  0.20% costs the busier versions drop to Sharpe 0.07-0.37.
- **It's a low-risk, low-return option, not a growth engine.** The Sharpe (0.96-1.14) is fine, but most of the
  return is the T-bill rate.
