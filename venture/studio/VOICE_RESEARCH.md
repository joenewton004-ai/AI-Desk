# Voice research: your own voice or an AI voice? (Backtest Bench)

Research date: 2026-11-01. Researched by Claude (research agent). Nothing bought, no accounts created.

**How to read this.** Every claim has a dated source. In this session the network blocked direct page fetches
(support.google.com, techcrunch.com, elevenlabs.io, openai.com, azure.microsoft.com, huggingface.co,
artificialanalysis.ai). What it could reach were search-engine extracts of those pages. So "YouTube Help says X"

---

## Short answer

1. **AI narration is not banned from monetisation.** I found no source for an "October 2026" ban on AI voices or
   visuals. The real change was on **16 July 2026**, and for a **finance** channel it matters a lot: YouTube now
   refuses to monetise channels that use **AI-generated personas to deliver information on sensitive topics, finance
   included**.
2. **For a finance channel, your own voice removes that risk completely.** It costs £0 and does not need the AI
   label. The research doesn't say a good AI voice teaches worse than a person. It does say people trust and engage
   with a human voice a bit more, especially once they know the other voice is AI.
3. **Recommendation:** narrate it yourself from week 1 and don't add a voice subscription. Keep a clone of your own
   voice as the fallback if recording gets in the way of the schedule. That costs about £5-£20/month and you only
   pay it if you need it. Run a free, small A/B test on Shorts (design in section 4) before paying for anything.

## 1. YouTube policy, November 2026

### 1a. Monetisation (YPP) and "inauthentic content"

What the YouTube Help Center says (channel monetisation policies, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392,
page as indexed by search in late 2026):

- **The rename.** On 15 July 2025, "repetitious content" became **"inauthentic content"**. The page calls this a
  clarification: mass-produced or repetitive content "has always been ineligible". The **reused content** policy
  (clips, compilations, reactions) did not change.
- **What is not monetisable:** content that "looks like it's made with a template" or feels repetitive across a
  channel's videos; "image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative,
  commentary, or educational value"; and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the
  impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective".
- **AI itself is allowed:** "Channels using AI can still be eligible for monetization, provided they follow
  YouTube's monetization policies." But a channel can still be rejected "if the content is not clearly original or if
  we cannot tell that you made the content".
- **The July 2026 clarification** (YouTube update rolled out 16 July 2026, reported by TechCrunch on 20 July 2026,
  https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/; Yahoo Finance
  syndication https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/off-putting-youtube-tighten-rules-104500810.html).
  It splits inauthentic content into **three** types that can't be monetised:
  1. generic, repetitive or template-based content;
  2. content designed to be distressing or emotionally manipulative;
  3. **AI personas discussing sensitive topics such as health, finance and legal issues.**

  It is judged **at channel level**: "channels found to carry too much of any of the three categories will lose the
  ability to monetize". YouTube didn't publish the threshold.
- **The Help Center wording of type 3** (answer/1311392, as indexed): "channels that use AI-generated personas to
  deliver information on sensitive topics ... any content that presents itself as a human expert providing advice to
  viewers on topics such as health, legal issues, finances, or politics ... channels uploading this content will not
  be allowed to monetize." One example given: "AI-generated podcast hosts offering financial guidance, investment
  tips, or wealth management advice."
- **The Spam policy** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973) adds that "using automated tools or AI to
  churn out high volumes of similar content with minimal changes" isn't allowed, and that disclosing AI "is not a
  free pass to violate our policies".

**Would an AI-narrated, faceless Backtest Bench be monetisable?**

- Being faceless and using an AI voice are not violations in themselves. Our format is original backtests, our own
  charts, and a script with an argument. That is the "educational value" and "original insight" the policy asks for.
- **The grey area is type 3.** We are a finance channel. The rule is aimed at AI that "presents itself as a human
  expert providing advice". We give education, not advice, and an unnamed narrator isn't presented as an expert, so
  we're probably outside it. But "probably" depends on how a reviewer sees it, and the threshold isn't public.
  A realistic AI voice that is never disclosed, talking about investing, is the closest our channel could come to
  that example. This is **my reading of the wording, not a YouTube ruling [unverified interpretation]**.
- **What gets channels demonetised in practice:** high-volume templated uploads, stock-footage slideshows with TTS
  read over them, near-identical Shorts, and AI "hosts" giving money tips. In January 2026 YouTube removed 16 of the
  100 largest "AI slop" channels identified by Kapwing (~4.7bn views). That figure comes from secondary reports only,
  e.g. https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage and
  https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/ **[primary YouTube statement not found]**. The
  same reports say some all-human faceless channels were hit as collateral. Creator blogs say the algorithm now
  favours faces and that in March 2026 YouTube tested a viewer survey asking "does this feel like AI slop?"
  **[secondary only]**.

**The "October 2026 headline" that AI voices and visuals are barred from monetisation: REFUTED as stated.** I found
no YouTube announcement, Help page or news report of a new ban in October 2026. The pages dated October 2026 that
search returned re-check the July rule, for example https://jawlah.co/en/60696 (19 Oct 2026, whose text matches the
July update) and https://elevate.uno/youtube-ai-content-policy (Help pages opened 16 Oct 2026). The most likely
source is a garbled retelling of the 16 July 2026 clarification. That clarification **does** bar AI personas on
finance topics, so the headline is wrong in general but **right about the bit that applies to us**.

### 1b. The altered or synthetic content label

YouTube Help, "Disclosing use of GenAI content" (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), and the YouTube
Blog (https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/, original 2023/24 announcement;
https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/, **27 May 2026**):

- **When it's required:** for **realistic** content that is meaningfully altered or synthetic, i.e. content a viewer
  could mistake for a real person, place or event. The examples include "synthetically generating a person's voice
  to narrate a video".
- **Not required:** content that is clearly unrealistic or animated, AI used for productivity (scripts, ideas,
  captions), and **"cloning your own voice to create voice overs or dubs"**, plus voice or audio repair.
- **27 May 2026 changes:** the label now sits directly under the player instead of hidden in the description.
  YouTube now **auto-applies** it when its systems detect significant photorealistic AI the creator didn't disclose.
  People who repeatedly fail to disclose can lose content or YPP. Coverage quotes YouTube saying labels "do not affect
  how our videos are recommended or whether they can earn money"
  (https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260528-youtube-ai-label/ and others, 28 May 2026).

**Does a stock AI narrator voice (not copying a real person) need the label?** **Unclear: the official wording
doesn't settle it.**
- The example is "a person's voice". Most guides read that as an identifiable real person, so a generic,
  invented narrator voice doesn't strictly need the label (e.g. https://minimatters.com/youtube-altered-or-synthetic-content-disclosure/,
  https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-disclosure-in-2026-label-it-yourself-or-youtube-will). **[Interpretation,
  not YouTube wording.]**
- Our plan's ground rules already say we disclose where required, and the label doesn't affect earnings. If we use
  any AI voice, **tick the box and say it in the description.** There are three reasons. It costs nothing. Since May
  2026 YouTube may auto-label us anyway. And being open is our best defence against the "AI persona posing as a human
  expert" rule. (Most of YouTube's detection signals are visual, so whether voice-only gets auto-labelled is
  **[unverified]**.)
- **Your own voice:** no label. **A clone of your own voice:** no label (explicit exemption).
- If we used OpenAI's TTS, OpenAI's own terms separately require telling listeners the voice is AI-generated (see
  section 3).

## 2. Evidence: human versus synthetic voices

Evidence strength scale: **A** = peer-reviewed randomised experiment; **B** = peer-reviewed or conference
observational study on large real-platform data, or an industry study with a stated method; **C** = industry survey
or secondary report with no method shown; **D** = creator anecdote or vendor marketing.

### What the better studies say

| Finding | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Older work found a strong "voice effect": people learned better from a human voice than from a robotic machine voice. | Summarised in Craig & Schroeder (2017, *Computers & Education*); revisited by Dinçer, *J. Pedagogical Research* 6(3), May 2022, doi 10.33902/jpr.202214591 (https://doaj.org/article/a14435a205f54240bdd0b0db2fa06f91) | A (older TTS) |
| With a **modern** TTS engine the learning gap closed. In Craig & Schroeder (2017) the modern engine even gave better transfer, with similar credibility ratings. | Craig & Schroeder 2017 (as cited in https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1341358.pdf) | A, small lab samples |
| AI-generated lecture video (generated text, TTS voice and avatar) vs a recorded video, ~76 students randomised: **retention a bit better** with the AI video, transfer the same, trust and motivation slightly (not significantly) lower, **social presence significantly lower**. | Xu et al. 2025, *British J. Educational Technology* 56(4), doi 10.1111/bjet.13530 (https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13530); sample size from a secondary summary | A, one small study, vocabulary learning |
| AI-generated instructors: **same test scores, lower video engagement** than a human instructor. | IRRODL 2024 (https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/7815) | A/B |
| Voice quality didn't change performance but **did change trust** in a virtual tutor (low-quality TTS < high-quality TTS < human). | Chiou et al., "The impact of virtual human voice on learner trust" (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337416956) | A, lab |
| 21,541 TikTok videos from 3,647 creators: AI voice linked to **lower engagement** (likes about -5.4%, also comments and shares). | ICIS 2023 conference paper (https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2023/user_behav/user_behav/24) | B, observational |
| 554,252 TikTok videos: AI voice **helped less-experienced creators** but **hurt established creators** and those with a clear personal identity. | Zhou, Zhang & Lee, UBC/NUS working paper, reportedly under review at MIS Quarterly (https://blogs.ubc.ca/genemoolee/2022/07/06/how-does-ai-generated-voice-affect-online-content-creation-evidence-from-text-to-speech-adoption-in-tiktok/) | B, observational, not yet peer-reviewed |
| Blind test, 1,326 US radio listeners: **couldn't tell AI from human**, appeal about the same. Humour worked better with a human voice (33% vs 26% "funny"). After being told the voice was AI, 20% liked it less; after being told it was human, 48% liked it more. | Crowd React Media, reported by Radio World / Inside Radio (https://www.radioworld.com/news-and-business/research-firm-says-radio-audiences-cant-tell-difference-between-real-and-ai-voices; https://www.insideradio.com/free/study-finds-ai-voices-equal-humans-on-sound-not-trust/article_b8187d55-51da-49a3-8b40-ad1278cac6ad.html) | B/C, industry |
| Synthetic-voiced audio ads matched human-voiced ads on brand engagement and purchase intent (neuroscience measures). | WPP Media / Choreograph / MediaScience (https://www.wppmedia.com/thought-leadership/research-business-intelligence/ai-voices-audio-ads-research) | C, industry; ads, not education |
| "People trust a human voice far more than AI": 55% vs 23%. | Audacy Innovation Tracker, cited second-hand | C **[primary not seen]** |

### What it means for us

- **Comprehension and learning:** a high-quality modern AI voice is **about as good** as a human one (several A
  studies, all small).
- **Trust and connection:** the human voice has a **small but consistent edge**, and it grows once people know the
  voice is AI (A/B/C evidence). We will disclose, so for us that edge is real.
- **Engagement on short-video platforms:** observational studies point slightly towards humans, with a twist. AI
  voice helps unknown creators and hurts creators with a personal brand. Backtest Bench's pitch is "an honest
  person tested it". That sounds like a personal brand, which favours your own voice. **[my inference]**
- **No rigorous study tests finance or investing YouTube specifically.** Nothing I found compares human and AI
  narration on YouTube audience retention directly.

### Voice characteristics

- **Pace:** in 6.9 million edX MOOC viewing sessions, faster speakers held viewers longer. Summaries report up to
  ~254 wpm holding viewers about twice as long as speakers under ~166 wpm (Guo, Kim & Rubin 2014, ACM L@S, doi
  10.1145/2556325.2566239, https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf). The wpm figures come from
  secondary summaries **[check in paper]**. Strength B, observational: fast speakers may also be more enthusiastic.
  The same study found **short videos** and an **informal, personal** style held attention better. *Practical
  takeaway: talk briskly (about 160-180 wpm) and conversationally, and cut pauses in the edit.*
- **Warmth and enthusiasm:** adding enthusiasm cues to a synthetic voice improved learners' emotions and cognitive
  load (Liew et al. 2022, *Education and Information Technologies*, doi 10.1007/s10639-022-11255-6). Strength A, lab.
- **Accent (UK):** the Sutton Trust "Speaking Up" report (Nov 2022, YouGov/Savanta, May 2022;
  https://www.suttontrust.com/news-opinion/all-news-opinion/over-a-quarter-of-senior-professionals-from-working-class-backgrounds-have-been-singled-out-for-their-accent-in-the-workplace/)
  found the old **prestige** ranking still holds: received pronunciation and standard Scottish at the top, and
  industrial-city accents (Birmingham, Liverpool, Manchester) lower. That measures **prestige, not trust or
  retention**. I found no study showing a regional accent loses viewers on YouTube. Strength B for prestige; no
  evidence on retention. *Practical takeaway: speak naturally and clearly. Don't put on an accent; sounding natural
  is part of the trust case.*
- **Pitch:** I found no source in this session. Leave it out. **[not researched]**

### Weak evidence: ignore

- A widely copied story of a "finance creator Maya R. @MoneyClarity" whose 5-second retention went 58% -> 79% after
  switching to her own voice appears only on Alibaba "product insights" pages, in two versions that contradict each
  other (https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/ai-generated-voiceovers-for-youtube-shorts-do-they-boost-retention-or-make-viewers-skip-faster.html).
  **Probably made up.** Strength D.
- Vendor blogs (Narration Box, Resemble, Fliki, ReadSpeaker and others) say AI voice is "fine" or "better". They
  sell AI voices. Strength D.
- Retention drops on AI-**dubbed** language tracks (https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/ai-dubbing-retention-3-youtube-case-studies)
  measure translated dubs, not original narration. Strength D, and a different question.

### Cloning your own voice (the middle option)

- **YouTube:** cloning your own voice for voiceovers is **explicitly exempt** from the altered-content label (Help
  14328491). Because it's you, it isn't an "AI persona" posing as someone else. That is how I read it; YouTube hasn't
  addressed it directly **[unverified interpretation]**.
- **Consent:** ElevenLabs only lets you make a **Professional** clone of **your own** voice, even if someone else
  agrees. It checks with a live "voice captcha", where you read a prompt aloud and it's matched to your samples
  (official help link above; Proof News on the captcha: https://proofnews.org/ai-tools-make-it-easy-to-clone-someones-voice-without-consent).
  Instant clones (Starter) need you to confirm you have the rights. Whether they also get a live check is reported
  inconsistently **[unverified]**. Never clone anyone else's voice. YouTube's impersonation and privacy rules also
  cover AI copies of someone's voice (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801947).
- **What it's good for:** pickups and fixes (re-saying a corrected number without re-recording), and keeping to the
  schedule on weeks you can't record. Starter's instant clone (~£4.50/month) is enough for pickups. A professional
  clone needs Creator (~£16.40/month) and about 30+ minutes of clean recordings **[check the current minimum]**.

## 4. Recommendation

**Decision: you narrate, from episode 1. Budget for voice: £0 now.**

Why:
1. **Policy risk.** On a finance channel, an AI narrator is the one setup that comes close to YouTube's July 2026
   "AI persona on finance" rule. Your own voice removes that risk and the labelling question in one go. Losing
   monetisation is a far bigger cost than any subscription.
2. **Trust is the product.** "Honest backtests" sells credibility. The evidence gives humans a small, consistent
   edge on trust and social presence, and shows AI voice hurting creators with a personal identity.
3. **Money.** £0 vs £16-£20 a month for full AI volume (~£200-£240 a year, half of what's left of the start-up cap).
   A recurring subscription with no proven benefit fails our money rules.
4. **Learning isn't at risk either way.** A good AI voice teaches about as well, so we lose nothing on clarity by
   choosing you, as long as you speak briskly and clearly.

**Fallback (only if needed):** if you miss the recording schedule in 2 weeks out of any 4, buy **ElevenLabs Starter
for one month** (~£4.50-£5.40). Make an instant clone of your own voice and use it for pickups and the Shorts. Ledger
it under Studio. Move up to Creator (~£16.40) only if the clone passes the test below and you really can't record.

### Cheap A/B test (cost £0)

YouTube's built-in "Test & compare" only tests **titles and thumbnails**, picking by watch time
(https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400). It can't test audio. Also, **don't upload two versions of the
same Short**: near-duplicates are exactly the "repetitive" content the policy targets, and the two would compete with
each other. Use matched pairs instead:

- **Step 0 - blind listening test (week 1, free):** record a 30-second clip yourself. Make the same clip with Google
  Chirp 3 HD (free tier) and an ElevenLabs British voice (free plan; fine for a private test). Play all three in
  random order to 8-10 people. Ask them to score 1-5 for "would keep watching", "sounds trustworthy" and "easy to
  follow", and to guess which is human. If your clip doesn't come last on "would keep watching", skip the on-platform
  test and just record yourself.
- **Step 1 - on-platform test (only if step 0 is close, or you'd rather not record):** **10 pairs of Shorts**
  (20 Shorts, ~7 weeks at 3 a week). Each pair = two different backtest topics that are as similar as possible, with
  the same template, length (±5 s), visuals, and posting slot. Toss a coin to decide which Short in the pair gets the
  AI voice. Use the ElevenLabs clone of **your** voice, or a stock British voice with the label ticked. Post the pair
  on alternate days in the same time slot.
- **Metrics (YouTube Studio, read at 7 days after posting, Shorts tab):**
  1. **Viewed vs swiped away / "stayed to watch"** - main metric (does the voice hold the first seconds?)
  2. **Average percentage viewed**
  3. **Subscribers gained per 1,000 engaged views**
  4. Comments mentioning the voice (count them)

  Compare **rates, not raw views**: Shorts views vary 10-100x for reasons that have nothing to do with the voice.
- **Minimum sample and decision rule (set now, before results):** for each pair, see which voice won on the main
  metric. By chance alone, one voice wins 8 or more of 10 pairs about 5% of the time (sign test). So:
  - **AI is adopted** only if it wins **at least 8 of 10 pairs** on "stayed to watch" **and** isn't worse on average
    percentage viewed.
  - Anything less: **human stays.** A tie is a win for the human, because the human is free and carries no policy risk.
  - Honest limit: 10 pairs only detect **big** differences. A small AI advantage would go unseen. That's fine: a
    small advantage wouldn't justify ~£200 a year plus the policy risk.
- **Long videos:** don't test voices on them. One a week is too few, and switching narrator mid-series confuses
  viewers. Long videos stay in your voice.

### Kill rule

Stop using AI voice immediately, and cancel any subscription before its renewal date, if **any** of these happens:
1. The A/B test doesn't reach 8 of 10 pairs for AI (decided at pair 10; no extending the test to fish for a result).
2. Any YouTube warning, YPP rejection or reviewer note that mentions inauthentic content, AI personas or the label.
3. 3 or more comments in a month calling the voice robotic or fake.
4. Voice spending goes over **£20 in total** before the channel has 300 subscribers (the plan's day-90 target).

If you narrate and the channel misses the day-90 Studio target (< 300 subscribers or < 20k Shorts views, PLAN.md),
the voice probably isn't the cause, so don't "fix" it by switching to AI. Look at topics, hooks and thumbnails first.
